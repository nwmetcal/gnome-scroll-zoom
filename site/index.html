Scroll Zoom

Zoom your whole screen the way a Mac does: hold Super and scroll. Smooth, it follows your pointer, and text stays sharp. A free accessibility extension for GNOME.

Install Scroll Zoom Source code

Why this exists

Scroll Zoom is made by a visually impaired developer who relies on screen zoom every day, and was tired of zoom tools that fall short on every platform. GNOME's built-in zoom works, but it jumps between levels, lags behind the pointer on fast displays, and blurs the very text you zoomed in to read. Scroll Zoom fixes those three things.

What it does

  • Hold Super and scroll to zoom in and out around the pointer, with a mouse wheel or a touchpad. Zoom all the way out and zoom turns off.
  • Zoom glides instead of jumping. Spin the wheel faster to zoom faster; a quick touchpad swipe coasts to a stop.
  • The view follows your pointer smoothly, in step with your display, even at 144 Hz and above.
  • Text stays sharp. Letters and lines are redrawn with crisp, smooth edges, while photos stay smooth.
  • It works with GNOME's own zoom: its keyboard shortcuts, Settings page, color effects, and crosshairs all keep working.

Install

You need GNOME 50 on Wayland, for example Ubuntu 26.04 or Fedora 44. To check, open a terminal and run gnome-shell --version.

From extensions.gnome.org, which keeps it up to date

  1. Install Extension Manager, if you don't have it. On Ubuntu, open a terminal and run:
    sudo apt install gnome-shell-extension-manager
    On other systems, get it from Flathub.
  2. Open Extension Manager, choose Browse, and search for Scroll Zoom.
  3. Choose Install…, then Install when GNOME asks. It starts right away, with no need to log out.

Then hold Super, the key with the Windows logo on most keyboards, and scroll.

You can also install it from extensions.gnome.org in your web browser: search for Scroll Zoom there and switch it on, with the GNOME Shell integration add-on the site offers.

How updates arrive

  • Each time you log in, and once a day after that, GNOME asks extensions.gnome.org for a newer Scroll Zoom that runs on your version of GNOME.
  • When there is one, GNOME downloads it and shows a notification, Extension Updates Available. The Extensions app says the extension will be updated on next login.
  • The new version starts the next time you log in. There is nothing to click or install.
  • GNOME looks for updates only while Extension Manager or the Extensions app is installed, so keep one of them.
  • A new version reaches extensions.gnome.org once the site's reviewers have checked it, which can take a few days.
  • After you upgrade GNOME, Scroll Zoom stays off until a version for the new GNOME is out. GNOME then installs it at a login, on its own.

From the release zip, which does not update itself

A copy installed this way, or from the source code, never updates itself: to get a new version, you download and install it again.

  1. Download the extension. It saves to your Downloads folder.
  2. Open a terminal and install it:
    gnome-extensions install --force ~/Downloads/scroll-zoom.shell-extension.zip
  3. Log out and log back in. GNOME only loads extensions installed this way when you log in.
  4. Open the Extensions app and switch on Scroll Zoom, or run:
    gnome-extensions enable scroll-zoom@nhicc

To switch to a copy that updates itself, uninstall this one, install Scroll Zoom from extensions.gnome.org as above, then log out and back in. The uninstall command is under Questions.

How to use it

Keys and what they do
Do thisTo
Hold Super and scroll up or downZoom in or out around the pointer
Zoom all the way outTurn zoom off
Super+Alt+8Turn zoom on or off, at the last level you used
Super+Alt+= and Super+Alt+-Zoom in and out with the keyboard
Super+Alt+\Switch smoothing off (hard pixels) and back on

These are the same keys a Mac uses, with Super in place of Cmd and Alt in place of Option. To change the settings, open the Extensions app and choose Scroll Zoom's settings: the keys to hold, how fast it zooms, the maximum zoom, and how text is smoothed.

Questions

Nothing happens when I hold Super and scroll

Log out and back in after installing. If it still does nothing, run gnome-extensions info scroll-zoom@nhicc: it should say State: ACTIVE. If it doesn't, tell us what journalctl --user -b -g scroll-zoom prints.

Can I use Ctrl and scroll, like on a Mac?

No. On GNOME, a scroll goes straight to the app under the pointer, and GNOME itself only sees it while Super is held. That's also why apps keep their own Ctrl and scroll zoom.

Text looks too bold or too thin, or I want to see real pixels

Choose another smoothing style in the settings, or press Super+Alt+\ to switch smoothing off.

Does it work over full-screen games, video, and virtual machines?

Yes. Its tests zoom over full-screen apps, both newer Wayland apps and the X11 apps most games are, over apps that take over the keyboard's shortcuts as virtual machines and remote desktops do, and over apps that keep drawing, as games and video do.

A game that locks and hides the pointer, as first-person games do, holds the pointer in one place, so zoom stays on that spot while you look around; the tests can't run such a game yet (issue 2). And while zoom is on, GNOME draws a full-screen game through its compositor instead of straight to the screen, which can cost a little speed.

Does it work on other GNOME versions?

It's built and tested on GNOME 50 under Wayland. New versions are added once its tests pass on them, and a copy installed from extensions.gnome.org then updates itself to it.

How do I remove it?
gnome-extensions uninstall scroll-zoom@nhicc
